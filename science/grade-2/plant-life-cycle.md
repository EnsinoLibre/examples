# Plant Life Cycle (Grade 2)

**Subject:** Science
**Grade:** 2
**Topic:** Life cycles, plants

## Learning objective

Students will be able to name and put in order the main stages of a plant's life cycle, from seed to a mature plant that makes new seeds.

## Background for students

Plants go through stages as they grow, just like people grow from babies into children and then adults. Here are the main stages of a plant's life cycle:

1. **Seed** - A seed is small and looks like it isn't doing anything, but it holds everything the plant needs to start growing.
2. **Sprout** - When a seed gets water, warmth, and sits in soil, it cracks open and a tiny root and stem push out. This is called sprouting, or germination.
3. **Seedling** - The sprout grows into a seedling: a small young plant with a thin stem and its first little leaves.
4. **Mature plant** - The seedling keeps growing taller and stronger, grows more leaves, and becomes a mature plant.
5. **Flower and fruit** - Many mature plants grow flowers. Inside the flower, new seeds form, often inside a fruit. When those seeds fall or are planted, the whole cycle starts again.

## Activity 1: Put the stages in order

Below are the five stages of the plant life cycle, listed out of order. Write the numbers 1 through 5 next to each one to show the correct order, starting with 1 for what happens first.

_____ Mature plant

_____ Seed

_____ Flower and fruit (new seeds form)

_____ Seedling

_____ Sprout

## Activity 2: Fill in the blank

Use the word bank to fill in each blank. Each word is used only once.

**Word bank:** seed, sprout, seedling, flower, roots

1. A __________ holds everything a plant needs to begin growing.
2. When a seed cracks open and a tiny root and stem appear, we call this a __________.
3. A young plant with a thin stem and its first small leaves is called a __________.
4. A plant's __________ grow underground and take in water.
5. Many mature plants grow a __________, where new seeds can form.

## Short questions

**1.** What does a seed need to sprout? Name at least two things.

**2.** Why is the seed stage important, even though it looks like nothing is happening?

**3.** What happens after a flower forms on a mature plant?

**4.** Draw a picture of your favorite stage of the plant life cycle and label it.

## Answer key

**Activity 1 (order):**
4 - Mature plant
1 - Seed
5 - Flower and fruit
3 - Seedling
2 - Sprout

**Activity 2 (fill in the blank):**
1. seed
2. sprout
3. seedling
4. roots
5. flower

**Short questions:**
1. Accept any two of: water, warmth, soil, sunlight (light is more important later for growth than for sprouting, so water, warmth, and soil are the core answers).
2. Accept answers along the lines of: the seed already contains everything the plant needs to start growing, it just needs the right conditions to begin.
3. New seeds form, often inside a fruit, and those seeds can grow into new plants, starting the cycle again.
4. Open-ended, check that the drawing matches a real stage and is labeled correctly.

## Teacher notes

**Time needed:** About 20 minutes, or 30 minutes if you add a class discussion or a real seed-planting demonstration alongside it.

**Before class, review:**
- If possible, bring in a real seed (a bean seed works well and is easy to see) so students can hold one before doing the activity.
- Some students may want to say the cycle starts with the flower instead of the seed. Both are true in a way, since the cycle repeats, but for this worksheet keep "seed" as the starting point to keep the ordering activity consistent.
- This pairs well with a simple hands-on follow-up: planting bean seeds in a cup with a wet paper towel so students can watch real sprouting over the following one to two weeks.
