# Reading Comprehension: The Oldest Tree in the Park (Grade 5)

**Subject:** Language Arts
**Grade:** 5
**Topic:** Reading comprehension, informational text

## Learning objective

Students will read a short informational passage and answer questions that check literal understanding, vocabulary in context, and simple inference.

## Instructions for students

Read the passage below. Then answer the questions that follow in complete sentences unless the question asks you to circle an answer.

## Passage

**The Oldest Tree in the Park**

At the edge of Miller's Park stands an oak tree that is over two hundred years old. The people in town call it the Watcher, because it has stood in the same spot since long before the park existed, back when the land was only fields and forest.

The Watcher survived a lightning strike almost fifty years ago. You can still see the scar, a long dark stripe that runs down one side of its trunk. Instead of killing the tree, the strike seemed to make it stronger. New branches grew out from the scar in the years after, thick with leaves every spring.

Scientists who study old trees, called dendrochronologists, can estimate a tree's age by counting the rings inside its trunk. Each ring usually means one year of growth. A wide ring means the tree had a good year, with plenty of rain and sunlight. A narrow ring means a harder year, maybe a drought or a cold season.

The town added a small metal fence around the Watcher's roots last year. The fence keeps people from walking too close and packing down the soil, which can make it hard for a tree's roots to get water and air. Now visitors can still admire the tree, just from a few steps back.

Today, families visit the Watcher to have picnics nearby, and every autumn the town hangs a string of paper lanterns in its lowest branches for the harvest festival. The tree that once stood alone in an empty field is now the most photographed spot in the whole park.

## Questions

**1.** How old is the Watcher, according to the passage?

Answer: _______

**2.** Why do people call the tree "the Watcher"? (short answer)

**3.** What happened to the tree about fifty years ago?

A) It was cut down and replanted
B) It was struck by lightning
C) It stopped growing
D) It was moved to the park

**4.** Based on the passage, what does the word "dendrochronologists" most likely mean?

A) People who plant new trees
B) People who study the age of trees using tree rings
C) People who build fences around trees
D) People who photograph parks

**5.** Why did the town build a fence around the tree's roots? (short answer, use details from the passage)

**6.** What can you infer about how the town feels about the Watcher? Support your answer with at least one detail from the passage.

---

## Answer key

1. Over two hundred years old.
2. Accept answers along the lines of: because it has stood in the same spot for so long, watching over the park and the land before it, since long before the park existed.
3. B) It was struck by lightning
4. B) People who study the age of trees using tree rings
5. To keep people from walking too close and packing down the soil, which makes it harder for the roots to get water and air.
6. Accept any reasonable inference supported by a detail, for example: the town cares about and values the tree, shown by building a protective fence, decorating it for the harvest festival, and calling it by a special name.

## Teacher notes

**Time needed:** About 25-30 minutes: 10 minutes to read (or read aloud), 15-20 minutes to answer questions and discuss.

**Before class, review:**
- Preview the word "dendrochronologists" with the class before reading if you think it will trip students up, though question 4 is designed to let them figure it out from context.
- Question 6 is the most open-ended one. There is no single correct answer, so look for a reasonable inference paired with a real detail from the text, not just a guess.
- This works well as a partner-read: one student reads a paragraph aloud, then switches, before everyone answers independently.
