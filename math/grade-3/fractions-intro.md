# Fractions Intro (Grade 3)

**Subject:** Math
**Grade:** 3
**Topic:** Introduction to fractions

## Learning objective

Students will be able to identify a fraction shown in a picture, compare simple fractions with the same denominator, and solve short word problems involving fractions of a group.

## Instructions for students

Look at each problem carefully. Write your answer in the blank. If a problem asks you to compare two fractions, use `<`, `>`, or `=`.

## Problems

**1.** A pizza is cut into 4 equal slices. 1 slice has been eaten. What fraction of the pizza is left?

Answer: _______

**2.** Look at this row of 6 stars: ★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

What fraction of the stars are shaded (★)?

Answer: _______

**3.** Compare the fractions: 2/5 _____ 4/5

**4.** Compare the fractions: 3/8 _____ 3/4

(Hint: draw two same-size rectangles and shade each fraction if you get stuck.)

**5.** Maria has 8 crayons. 3 of them are broken. What fraction of her crayons are broken?

Answer: _______

**6.** A class of 10 students voted for their favorite fruit. 5 students chose apples. What fraction of the class chose apples? Write the fraction in its simplest form.

Answer: _______

**7.** Draw a circle and divide it into 3 equal parts. Shade 2 of the parts. What fraction did you shade?

Answer: _______

**8.** Word problem: Tomás has a chocolate bar with 12 equal squares. He gives 4 squares to his friend. What fraction of the chocolate bar did he give away?

Answer: _______

---

## Answer key

1. 3/4
2. 3/6 (or simplified: 1/2)
3. 2/5 < 4/5
4. 3/8 < 3/4
5. 3/8
6. 5/10, simplified to 1/2
7. 2/3
8. 4/12, simplified to 1/3

## Teacher notes

**Time needed:** About 20-25 minutes, including time for students to draw their own shapes in problems 4 and 7.

**Before class, review:**
- Make sure students already understand that a fraction's bottom number (denominator) tells how many equal parts make a whole, and the top number (numerator) tells how many of those parts you have.
- Problems 3 and 4 are a good moment to check whether students understand that with the same numerator, a bigger denominator means smaller pieces (many students guess this backwards at first).
- Simplifying fractions (problems 6 and 8) is optional depending on where your class is at. If your students haven't covered simplifying yet, accept 5/10 and 4/12 as correct answers.
- Have some paper or fraction strips on hand in case a few students want to draw the pizza, chocolate bar, or circles instead of just visualizing them.
